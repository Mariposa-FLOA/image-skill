# Symbolic Narrative Poster

Create a poster that can be described as one complete visual sentence. A title may clarify the sentence, but may not be its only idea.

This Skill is for **conceptual compression**: translate a real tension in the supplied or chosen subject into one altered relation between image, field, and type. The result should feel like an authored cultural poster—spare, clear, and memorable at thumbnail size.

Its signature mechanism is **semantic metamorphosis**: a real visual fact from the subject (a shadow, road line, garment fold, railing, wave, hair, architectural seam, reflection, or horizon) begins credibly, then becomes the graphic territory that carries the idea. The transformation must happen once, cleanly, and visibly. The two successful reference modes are: **continuation** (a fact grows into a path, plane, or field) and **absence** (a removed wall, gap, or missing unit becomes the subject of the poster). Other modes are allowed only when they are equally concrete.

Do not use it for cinematic campaign key art, generic editorial layout, material collage, retro print simulation, or a portrait with decorative typography.

## V2 Art-Director Preflight

The metaphor must be visible before it is named. Lock one tension, one operation, one anchor, one dominant field, and one type measure. Keep the source fact believable long enough to be recognised, then transform it once; do not stack multiple symbols, props, frames, gradients, or effects.

Before rendering, perform four checks:

- **thumbnail:** transformation first, anchor second, type third;
- **swap:** replacing the source breaks the meaning;
- **grayscale:** hierarchy survives without hue;
- **clean master:** broad fields are smooth and quiet, with no grain, dirt, decorative microcopy, fake paper wear, or visual noise by default.

If the result reads as “a colour grade plus text,” restart with a new source fact or composition—not a larger title. If the user rejects it, change the operation, dominant mass, anchor scale, and type behaviour before making another render.

## Find the visual sentence

Read the source for a concrete tension: distance, repetition, obstruction, passage, imbalance, absence, containment, waiting, split attention, arrival, or drift. Select one tension only.

Turn it into a single visible operation:

- **divide**: one space or object is cleanly separated into incompatible states;
- **contain**: a figure, object, or horizon is held by a boundary that changes its meaning;
- **measure**: scale, count, or type makes an otherwise invisible pressure visible;
- **interrupt**: an image is stopped or redirected by one consequential cut, band, gap, or threshold;
- **reverse**: foreground and background trade roles, revealing an unexpected absence;
- **erode**: a form is reduced by a single controlled subtraction, not texture.

The operation must arise from the source. Do not introduce a random mirror, rope, weapon, vehicle, architectural prop, or symbolic object merely to force a concept.

Before generating, state silently: “This poster says ___ through ___.” If that sentence is vague, choose another operation. Also name the handoff: “the source fact is ___; it becomes ___.” If the two halves cannot be described without adding a random prop, do not generate yet.

If no source image is supplied, choose a simple subject whose physical evidence can support the sentence; do not pretend an invented object came from a source. The generated scene still needs one believable fact to transform before it becomes graphic.

## Compose one decisive relation

- Build around one dominant mass, one anchor, and one interruption. Do not combine multiple metaphors.
- Reduce the photograph or illustration decisively: crop, isolate, silhouette, scale down, merge with a field, or place it at a threshold. Preserve the source’s recognisable facts where provided.
- Use generous negative space only when it creates pressure, distance, or scale. Blank space without a role is not minimalism.
- Keep the first read structural: a split, loop, horizon, imbalance, missing section, gate, measurement, or collision. The viewer should grasp it before reading the title.
- Let type participate physically: it may measure a boundary, extend a seam, complete a missing edge, or be interrupted by the same operation. Do not just place a title beneath the image.

### Metamorphosis control

When using the signature mechanism, make the handoff legible:

1. Begin from a believable source fact close to the anchor.
2. Preserve that fact long enough for the viewer to recognise it.
3. Let it broaden, flatten, taper, or continue into one unambiguous graphic field.
4. Make the destination field alter the subject's reading—path, distance, boundary, pull, or absence—not just fill empty space.

The anchor should occupy roughly one eighth to one quarter of the poster in a transformation-led layout. Give the transformed field enough scale to become the first read, but do not let it make the figure arbitrary. If a person is supplied, preserve their identity and body logic with restrained editorial cleanup.

Use one of these two layout families when it fits the evidence:

- **Continuation**: start with a believable line, contour, or shadow at the anchor; let it widen or extend until it becomes the dominant field. The starting portion must remain visibly connected to the destination.
- **Absence**: remove one measured architectural or patterned unit; make the resulting void carry the subject. The void must have a crisp boundary and a clear scale relationship to a small anchor.

Do not combine continuation and absence in the same poster. This is a choice of visual grammar, not a list of effects.

Read [references/visual-sentences.md](references/visual-sentences.md) when selecting a metaphor or rebuilding after a weak test.

## Art-direction pass

Before generation, lock five decisions:

1. **Sentence:** one tension and one transformation, written in plain language.
2. **Anchor:** the one person, object, or place that proves the sentence; it must remain recognisable.
3. **Dominant mass:** the field, void, line, or plane that carries the idea; it must be visible at thumbnail size.
4. **Value and colour:** neutral field first, anchor second, one controlled conflict colour third. Each colour needs a job.
5. **Type measure:** the exact edge, gap, baseline, or interval that the title will follow.

Use deliberate asymmetry or a measured rhythm. A large empty area is acceptable only when it establishes scale, distance, or pressure; do not use emptiness to hide an underdeveloped composition. Avoid default centered subjects, evenly spaced template cards, arbitrary rounded frames, and symmetrical layouts without a meaningful break.

For a vertical poster, keep the anchor and title in a safe margin while allowing the dominant structure to reach or crop at an edge. Do not force every element into the centre or the bottom title zone.

## Colour and finish

- Use two or three major colours plus a neutral. Give each a job: field, anchor, conflict, or type.
- Prefer clean matte fields, controlled tonal separation, and sharp but natural edges. Preserve skin and object credibility if a real person/object appears.
- Start from a clear contemporary palette, not automatic teal/orange, sepia, black/red thriller, or a nostalgic LUT.
- Default to no grain, dust, paper wear, halftone, fake microtext, faux credits, random labels, lens effects, or texture overlays.
- Treat the outcome as a precise screenprint-like flat image only in its clarity—not in simulated damage or vintage dirt.

## Typography

Use exact supplied copy. If none is supplied, create a restrained, original title of one to three words. Avoid invented brands, awards, dates, locations, and credits.

- Choose a plain, legible sans or serif with deliberate proportion.
- Use title scale and placement to resolve the visual operation. It should never be a generic large word behind a person.
- Keep supporting copy absent unless it materially improves the visual sentence.

For the approved layout families, use the corresponding type behavior:

- In **continuation**, set the title on the transformed path/edge or let its baseline extend the movement.
- In **absence**, set the title against or inside the void so its spacing measures the missing unit.

The title should explain the tension only after the image has established it. Do not ask typography to rescue an unclear transformation.

## Inspect and repair the generated image

Review the first result as a designer, not as a prompt match:

- At thumbnail size, does the transformation arrive before the title?
- Is the anchor still a believable person/object rather than a sticker?
- Does the dominant field have a purposeful edge and a clear value hierarchy?
- Is the type measuring or completing the relation, rather than labeling it afterward?
- Are there defaults—vignette, muddy blacks, fake grain, random microcopy, decorative props, or generic gradients—that were not part of the sentence?

If any answer fails, make one targeted edit that preserves the concept and fixes the specific failure. If the failure is structural, regenerate from the blank plane with a different crop or dominant mass; do not stack effects onto a weak layout.

## Boundaries

- Use `cinematic-key-art-poster` for a film-world promise, genre, dramatic lighting, or ensemble/story key art.
- Use `graphic-composition-poster` when photo, grid, crop, and typography form an editorial layout without a narrative metaphor.
- Use this Skill when the image itself is transformed into a single conceptual statement.

## Reset gate

If the result reads as “a colour grade plus text,” a decorative photo, a random symbolic prop, a generic person on a coloured shape, or a reference imitation, restart with a different operation. Changing typeface, adding grain, or adding another object does not fix a weak sentence.

## Quality gate

Before delivery, verify:

- The entire poster has one readable visual sentence.
- The visual operation is evident without the title.
- The handoff from source fact to graphic field is physically legible in one glance.
- The anchor, field, and type share one edge, measurement, or interruption.
- The chosen family is singular: continuation or absence, never both.
- The image survives a thumbnail check: transformation first, anchor second, type third.
- The palette is clean and limited; no artificial grit hides weaknesses.
- Nothing resembles a copy of a reference’s exact composition, palette, title treatment, or punchline.
- It is materially distinct from existing cinematic, Y2K, collage, and graphic-composition Skills.

## Format and delivery

- When no ratio is specified, inspect the source fact, transformed field, and negative space and choose the canvas that makes the visual sentence clearest. Follow an explicit user ratio only when one is supplied; never stretch the result to fit.
- Keep type and anchor within safe margins.
- Do not archive a test unless the user accepts it. For accepted work, save under `./accepted-outputs/01-我们的Skill/01-自主原创Skill/` in its own visual-language folder, without overwriting an earlier result.
- Return the final image, status (`test` or `accepted`), used Skill, selected visual sentence, and saved absolute path only when archived.
