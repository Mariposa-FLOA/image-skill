# Visual System

Use this reference after selecting the spatial route.

## Composition system

Build the poster from one grid and one directed eye path. The grid may be strict or optically adjusted, but image crop, subject, primary type, supporting copy, horizon, and major plane edges must share alignments.

Use three beats:

- **entry:** the first-read silhouette, face, object, word, or counterform;
- **collision:** the point where type and image, or two planes, physically interact;
- **release:** a quiet field, open edge, or distant exit that prevents the poster from becoming a filled collage.

Crop with intent. A large crop must amplify identity, material, gaze, or movement; it cannot merely enlarge the source. Keep at least one meaningful off-frame continuation when it strengthens motion or scale.

## Mass and axis

Begin with three unequal masses: dominant spatial field, subject or structural counterweight, and accent. A useful starting ratio is 60/30/10, not a rule.

Choose one dominant axis and one counter-axis:

- vertical section versus horizontal room band;
- deep perspective versus flat graphic void;
- diagonal fold versus stable subject;
- radial module field versus one quiet edge;
- heavy lower plane versus suspended upper fragment.

Avoid accidental central symmetry, evenly spaced modules, and interest distributed across every corner.

## Color stances

Choose color from the source and give each hue a spatial role:

- high-key mineral field + deep neutral architecture + one saturated threshold;
- near-monochrome environment + source-colored subject;
- cool structural planes + warm reflected human zone;
- warm architectural field + one cold synthetic void;
- black and off-white structure + one source-derived chromatic edge;
- clean daylight naturalism + an impossible colored shadow;
- dense single hue + pale cutaway interior.

Do not default to cyan/orange, dark navy/amber, beige editorial minimalism, or the same palette across unrelated sources.

Treat color as structure rather than a full-image filter. Assign a dominant field, a counterfield, and one source-derived accent. A time, temperature, or reality shift must be contained by a real letterform, plane, shadow, window, or threshold; do not wash the whole image with two competing grades.

## Light architecture

Define one dominant source and one spatial response:

- hard sun + folded cast shadow;
- overcast sky + color spill from a threshold;
- window light + reflected room plane;
- direct flash + flat modular separation;
- practical interior pool + dark exterior void;
- clean graphic light when the world is intentionally non-photographic.

All transformed planes must agree on time of day. Use shadow direction to explain orientation; do not add rim light to every edge.

## Typography behaviors

Choose one:

- title aligned to a real structural edge;
- title projected across one uninterrupted plane;
- title occupying a deliberately missing room or void;
- compact sectional label outside the image body;
- one letter interrupted by the same fold or threshold as the space;
- title scale contrasting with a tiny subject or module.

When the graphic engine leads, additionally choose one image-type relationship:

- the subject passes behind and then in front of one letter system;
- the counterforms reveal a different crop, time, material, or viewpoint;
- a structural feature in the photograph completes a letter stroke;
- the word is split by the same threshold that changes the space;
- repeated letterforms establish rhythm while one source anchor breaks the sequence.

Use at least two deliberate depth states when type and photography overlap. A word printed entirely above the image in clean sky is supporting copy, not an integrated poster action.

Keep copy minimal. Use an original 1–4 word title and one restrained tagline only when the user supplies none. Never fabricate architects, studios, measurements, coordinates, issue numbers, awards, credits, or technical specifications.

## Material hierarchy

Allow detailed realism in at most two zones:

- identity-bearing person, product, or landmark;
- one structural material that explains the transformation.

Simplify sky, walls, floors, water, shadow, and voids into smooth planes or restrained gradients. Do not add grain or distress merely to imply editorial sophistication.

## Series variation

Across multiple inputs, vary route, dominant axis, module count, subject scale, title behavior, horizon height, and color stance. Preserve a shared visual universe only when the user requests a campaign.
