# Quality Gates

Inspect the complete poster at thumbnail size, normal viewing size, and 100% crop.

## Concept

- One sentence can explain the spatial contradiction without describing effects.
- At least three source-specific details remain visible or structurally encoded.
- Removing typography does not collapse the result into an ordinary photograph.
- The poster asks one deliberate question instead of presenting random novelty.
- The source appeal is felt before the spatial trick is decoded.
- The spatial act expresses an emotional turn; it is not a literal oversized illustration of a keyword.
- One active design verb explains the poster: overlap, cut, amplify, crop, interrupt, reveal, fold, or another precise action.
- Removing the supporting copy does not remove the main graphic idea.

## Perspective and construction

- Every plane has a readable orientation and edge condition.
- Vanishing behavior is coherent inside each perspective system.
- Thresholds explain where one system changes into another.
- Section cuts show plausible thickness; modules have credible volume.
- Contact, cast, and reflected shadows agree with the light source.
- No melted corners, floating bases, impossible joints, doubled rails, or unresolved seams.

## Subject integrity

- Faces, bodies, hands, products, vehicles, furniture, and landmarks remain recognizable and structurally coherent.
- A person is not stretched to match a plane.
- Repeated subjects preserve identity and clothing.
- Plane edges and typography do not accidentally cross faces, hands, or key labels.

## Hierarchy and typography

- One spatial event dominates at three meters.
- The eye follows a clear entry, turn, and exit.
- No more than five major planes or modules compete.
- Title and tagline are exact, legible, and rendered once.
- Widely spaced or perspective-set type is checked character by character.
- No fake technical data, arbitrary symbols, or accidental glyphs appear.
- Primary type and image visibly share structure through counterform, crop, edge, mask, depth, or spatial continuity.
- When typography leads, at least two intentional front/behind or inside/outside relationships are readable.

## Finish

- Broad planes are calm and free of crawling noise, grain, stippling, scratches, dirty masking, or HDR halos.
- Detail is concentrated in no more than two meaningful material zones.
- Blacks retain structure; highlights roll off cleanly.
- Subject, transformed structure, and background separate through value or color without outline effects.

## Rejection triggers

Redesign instead of polishing when the result is only:

- a tilted or fisheye source photo;
- a person standing on a floating rectangle;
- equal photo boxes with drop shadows;
- a generic spiral staircase or infinite corridor unrelated to the source;
- random architecture pasted behind a portrait;
- typography and coordinate decoration carrying the entire concept;
- a noisy 3D render with no preserved source identity.
- a straight vertical or horizontal seam presenting two photographs side by side without thickness, shared light, or a source-derived threshold;
- an unchanged foreground subject placed over an impossible background while the title provides most of the concept.
- a primary vehicle, garment, product, or landmark converted into a different object category without an explicit user request;
- a giant road, staircase, portal, room stack, or monument that makes the source subject feel like a prop;
- a technically clean result whose only readable idea is the spatial gimmick.
- a finished photograph with a large title placed in unused sky or margin;
- a small local effect that cannot carry the poster at thumbnail size;
- typography, color grading, and spatial effects operating as three unrelated layers.
