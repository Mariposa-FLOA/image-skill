# Cinematic Poster System

Read this file when selecting the narrative composition, genre treatment, color script, and type hierarchy.

## Composition routes

For every route, solve the wordless key art first. Choose at least two non-text transformations—spatial rewrite, scale shift, environmental transformation, symbolic integration, optical structure, montage, or omission. Title placement comes after the transformed visual premise is already readable.

### A. Character Monument

Use for one person with a strong face, stance, costume, or private emotional state.

- Make the person the dominant scale anchor.
- Use one supporting environmental clue or symbolic fragment, not a collage of unrelated scenes.
- Place the title in negative space created by gaze, shoulders, clothing, or silhouette.
- Favor restrained hierarchy: face or figure first, title second, tagline third.
- Change more than crop and grade: use transformed scale plus one source-derived environment, reflection, threshold, shadow, or symbol.

### B. Relationship Fracture

Use for two people whose distance, alignment, gaze, touch, or opposition carries the story.

- Preserve both identities and their relative importance.
- Use a seam, reflection, doorway, horizon, shadow, or color-temperature split to express the relationship.
- Avoid generic face-to-face symmetry when the source contains a more specific gesture.
- Let the title connect or divide the figures according to the emotional premise.
- Re-stage the spatial relationship or optical plane so the distance is visible before any title appears; a simple two-person still with a seam overlay is insufficient.

### C. World Threshold

Use for landscapes, architecture, vehicles, weather, or a small person facing a large environment.

- Make scale and entry direction tell the story.
- Keep one clear horizon, corridor, doorway, road, window, or light source as the visual path.
- A person may be small, but their silhouette and orientation must remain readable.
- Place the title as part of the world: low on the horizon, suspended in sky, or aligned to architecture.
- Turn the threshold into an active visual event through scale, light behavior, atmosphere, or a transformed passage—not merely a scenic background.

### D. Symbol Evidence

Use for an object, body detail, trace, sign, garment, or still life that can imply a larger story.

- Isolate one object and enlarge its texture or consequence.
- Add only a faint human or environmental trace when necessary.
- Use precise negative space and a compact title; mystery comes from omission, not random darkness.
- Do not invent crime evidence, blood, or danger unless present or requested.
- Alter scale and context so the object carries the story without relying on a caption.

### E. Ensemble Constellation

Use for three or more people with distinct roles.

- Establish a hierarchy of primary, secondary, and atmospheric figures.
- Keep each face separate and readable; never blend identities.
- Arrange by narrative relationship, not equal-sized floating heads.
- Use one shared light, shape, place, or direction to bind the ensemble.

## Genre lenses

Select one primary lens. A secondary influence may shape texture, but do not mix every genre.

### Prestige drama

- Naturalistic or sculpted light, patient negative space, muted color with one emotional accent.
- Refined serif, restrained grotesk, or carefully spaced title.
- Emphasize consequence, memory, solitude, or relationship without melodramatic effects.

### Psychological thriller

- Uneven framing, obstruction, controlled shadow, visual repetition, or one unsettling spatial contradiction.
- Condensed sans, sharp serif, or narrow uppercase title.
- Tension must come from the source's gaze, space, or object—not invented violence.

### Romance or intimacy

- Warm/cool interplay, tactile distance, soft environmental separation, or paired visual rhythm.
- Elegant serif or humanist sans; avoid decorative script unless supplied.
- Preserve ambiguity when the image suggests closeness but not an explicit romance.

### Science fiction or speculative

- Geometric scale, unfamiliar light behavior, restrained interface-like lines, reflective material, or transformed atmosphere.
- Geometric sans or custom letter spacing.
- Do not add spaceships, armor, planets, or technology without source support.

### Action or movement

- Strong diagonal, directional blur, compressed scale, bold silhouette, or environmental momentum.
- Heavy condensed sans with simple, high-impact hierarchy.
- Preserve physical plausibility; do not invent explosions or weapons by default.

### Documentary or observational

- Honest location detail, available-light feeling, contextual typography, and minimal compositing.
- Neutral sans, archival serif, or location-derived type rhythm.
- Avoid fictional spectacle; make specificity and human presence carry the poster.

## Color script

Choose one dominant emotional temperature and one counter-accent. Useful structures include:

- deep cool field + small warm human light;
- warm environment + cool isolated subject;
- near-monochrome + one symbolic color;
- daylight realism + one unnatural but restrained accent;
- black field + material highlight for object-led posters.

These are examples, not a default palette. Before choosing any of them, compare the recent outputs. If they already use dark cool fields, warm skin, amber horizons, or rust-red accents, choose a different source-supported color stance from `art-direction-system.md`.

Color should reveal hierarchy and emotion. Avoid preset grading that affects every source identically.

Finish with controlled poster color rather than a global cinematic LUT:

- protect skin exposure and hue even when the environment becomes stylized;
- set a readable black floor so dark clothing and architecture retain material detail;
- compress bright windows, sea glare, lamps, and sunset into soft highlight roll-off;
- separate foreground, subject, and world through local color temperature or saturation;
- keep the clean default free of grain, distress, and bloom unless the user requests an analog finish.

The grade must support a transformed visual premise. It cannot rescue an unchanged photograph.

## Type hierarchy

- Title: one unmistakable typographic event, integrated with image geometry.
- Tagline: one short supporting line, visually quieter than the title.
- Microcopy: optional, neutral, and factual; never fake a professional billing block.
- Chinese and English may coexist only when both are useful; do not auto-translate a title into a second decorative language.

Typography should reflect the story lens rather than a generic film font. Do not place long text over a detailed face or critical symbol.

## Series variation

Across a batch, vary composition route, title position, scale relationship, dominant temperature, visual symbol, and amount of negative space. Preserve a shared title system only when the user wants a coherent campaign.
