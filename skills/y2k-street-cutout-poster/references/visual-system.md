# Original Visual System

Read this file when choosing the page structure, palette, and text hierarchy for a poster.

## Composition families

### A. Lens Rush

Best for selfies, phones, hands, shoes, bags, or other objects pushed toward the camera.

- Keep the person as an irregular central cutout.
- Enlarge the nearest object as a secondary crop and let it cross the page edge.
- Place a burst, radial halftone, or offset echo behind the forward object.
- Set the headline away from the face, usually low-left or vertically along the opposite edge.

### B. Street Slice

Best for full-body poses, strong clothing silhouettes, stairways, alleys, elevators, or architectural lines.

- Divide the background into two or three asymmetric paper fields.
- Let the subject bridge the fields with a thick contrasting keyline or layered shadow.
- Use one vertical editorial strip and one compact headline block.
- Echo a real line from the setting with tape, stripes, a crop boundary, or condensed type.

### C. Close Signal

Best for close portraits, beauty details, jewelry, hair, or a strong expression.

- Keep one large face crop and one smaller repeated detail, never two competing full faces.
- Use an orbit, target, scan bar, or sticker cluster to frame rather than cover facial features.
- Place the headline in the largest quiet zone; microcopy may follow a curve or edge.
- Preserve more neutral space than the other families so the portrait remains premium.

### D. Double Exposure Editorial

Best for two people or a single subject with a strong reflection or shadow.

- Use one primary cutout and one offset monochrome echo.
- Separate the layers with registration drift, torn-paper overlap, or a narrow color channel.
- Give each figure a distinct reading direction; do not merge faces or limbs.
- Use paired copy: one main phrase and one answering phrase.

## Palette generator

Choose one pair and tune it to the source clothing. Add black, paper white, and at most one small metallic-gray accent.

- electric cyan + signal pink
- acid green + ultraviolet
- tangerine + ice blue
- cobalt + lemon
- scarlet + cool mint
- hot magenta + chrome gray

Do not reuse electric cyan + signal pink automatically. The subject's clothing and skin contrast should decide the palette.

## Texture grammar

Use two or three texture families, not all of them:

- coarse photocopy grain on a monochrome crop;
- halftone field or radial dots behind an object;
- dry toner scratches at the page edge;
- slight CMYK registration drift around one cutout;
- translucent tape, price-label shape, or rough sticker edge;
- scanned paper folds or worn ink, kept away from eyes and small text.

Texture should reveal the collage construction. It must not reduce the whole image to a dirty vintage filter.

## Type hierarchy

- Headline: tall condensed sans or heavy geometric grotesk; one dominant block.
- Secondary phrase: smaller neutral sans, preferably horizontal.
- Microcopy: issue number, short category, or editorial code; use sparingly.
- Decorative marks: dots, stars, brackets, underlines, or directional arrows; repeat one family for consistency.

Avoid long paragraphs, faux Japanese or Korean characters, random letter strings, copied magazine mastheads, and text over the eyes or mouth.

## Anti-template variation

For a batch, vary at least three of these across images: composition family, crop scale, background split, palette, headline position, enlarged detail, texture family, and text direction. Preserve one shared element only when the user requests a coherent series.
